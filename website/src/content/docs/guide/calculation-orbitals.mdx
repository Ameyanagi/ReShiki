xTB orbitals come from a minimal basis. Use them to see the shape and node structure of frontier orbitals, not for quantitative orbital energies.

## Show an orbital

Every GFN2-xTB and g-xTB run has orbitals (GFN-FF has none). After a [calculation](/guide/calculations/), open the **Orbitals** tab of the results palette. The **Orbitals** task on the Calculate card stays off: the tab replaces it.

<Screenshot
  src="/manual/xtb-orbitals.webp"

The HOMO is drawn as blue and orange lobes on the 3D model."
/>

- The **level diagram** shows occupied levels with ↑↓, marks HOMO, LUMO and the gap, and puts degenerate levels side by side.
- The **list** shows the levels around the gap with their energies in eV. Degenerate levels are marked, for example `HOMO · 2-fold`. **HOMO** and **LUMO** jump to the frontier orbitals.
- Click a level to show that orbital on the molecule's 3D model. After you click one, ↑ and ↓ move through the levels while nothing on the canvas is selected.
- Open shells show α and β columns, and a switch between them.

<Screenshot
  src="/manual/xtb-orbitals-open-shell.webp"
  alt="Triplet O2 (unpaired electrons 2, multiplicity 3). The Orbitals tab shows α and β columns in the level diagram, the α/β switch set to α, and one member of the 2-fold α π* HOMO pair drawn on the model."
/>

For xTB results, ReShiki recomputes the orbital when you show it (about 0.1 s for small molecules), so files stay small. A surface usually appears within a second.

## Adjust the surface

- **Isovalue**: 0.01 to 0.10 a.u. on the slider, with presets 0.02, 0.05 (default) and 0.10. If no surface is found, the tab says `No surface at this isovalue: lower it.`
- **Look**: **Solid**, **Shaded**, **Outline** or **Mesh**, plus **Mesh lines**.
- **Opacity**: 20 to 100 %.
- **Phases**: the two lobe colours follow the canvas theme.

## Place it in the drawing

A shown orbital is a preview: it is not saved and adds no Undo step. **Place on canvas** puts it in the drawing as a figure attached to the 3D model, in one Undo step. Changes to a placed figure, including moving to another level, are Undo steps too, and **Remove from canvas** takes it out.

<Screenshot
  src="/manual/xtb-orbital-placed.webp"
  alt="The benzene LUMO placed on the canvas. The status bar reads Placed LUMO on the canvas · ⌘Z removes it, and the tab shows Placed on the canvas and Remove from canvas."
/>

A placed orbital is a vector figure. It turns with the model, hides correctly behind atoms and bonds, and exports sharp to SVG and PDF without raster images.

<Screenshot
  src="/manual/xtb-orbital-pdf.webp"
  alt="A PDF exported from ReShiki with the benzene LUMO placed on its 3D model, next to the 2D structure."
/>

The file keeps what the figure needs, so it reopens with the same orbital and the same signs, even in builds without xTB and in `reshiki --cli convert`. A figure is drawn only while its result belongs to the molecule and the model shows that result's geometry; after an edit or on an earlier optimization step it is hidden.

## Orbitals from other programs

Opening a Molden or Gaussian fchk file attaches its results to the molecule and opens the palette, titled **Calculation results** with the file's program and method. The Orbitals tab then works as above. Orbitals from these files cannot be recomputed, so the file's coefficients are used directly. When a file's basis uses conventions ReShiki does not support yet (for example Cartesian d shells in fchk), it brings the levels without shapes and the import warnings say why. Gaussian logs and ORCA outputs bring orbital energies but no shapes.

## File size

By default, orbital coefficients and surfaces are not saved with xTB results; ReShiki recomputes them. A placed orbital saves its own coefficients and a drawing of its surface (up to about 300 KB). Imported results keep their orbitals while the imported data take no more than 2 MB per molecule. **Keep full results in this file** keeps everything.

## Limits

- A very thin lobe in a large molecule can fall between grid points and vanish; lower the isovalue.
- The editable copy of the drawing inside an exported PNG, SVG or PDF leaves calculation data out, so a placed orbital does not come back when you re-import the picture. The picture itself shows it.
