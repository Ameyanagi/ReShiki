---
title: How to cite
description: What to cite when you publish results from ReShiki's xTB calculations.
---

:::caution[Not released yet]
xTB calculations are in nightly builds and not in a released version of ReShiki yet.
:::

If ReShiki's calculations helped your work, please cite ReShiki, the axtb engine and the method you ran. Some features add a few papers; the table below lists the minimum. Every reference, with its DOI where there is one and what ReShiki uses it for, is on the [references page](https://github.com/Ameyanagi/ReShiki/blob/main/docs/references/xtb.md), and all entries are in one BibTeX file, [xtb.bib](https://github.com/Ameyanagi/ReShiki/blob/main/docs/references/xtb.bib).

## Always

- **ReShiki**, the release you used: [github.com/Ameyanagi/ReShiki](https://github.com/Ameyanagi/ReShiki)
- **axtb**, the xTB engine inside ReShiki: [github.com/atomsforhumanity/axtb](https://github.com/atomsforhumanity/axtb). Cite it for results ReShiki calculated.
- **The method:**
  - GFN2-xTB: C. Bannwarth, S. Ehlert, S. Grimme, _J. Chem. Theory Comput._ **15**, 1652 (2019), [doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.8b01176](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.8b01176)
  - GFN-FF: S. Spicher, S. Grimme, _Angew. Chem. Int. Ed._ **59**, 15665 (2020), [doi:10.1002/anie.202004239](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202004239); for lanthanides and actinides also T. Rose, M. Bursch, J.-M. Mewes, S. Grimme, _Inorg. Chem._ **63**, 19364 (2024), [doi:10.1021/acs.inorgchem.4c03215](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.4c03215)
  - g-xTB: T. Froitzheim, M. Müller, A. Hansen, S. Grimme, ChemRxiv preprint (2025), [doi:10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-bjxvt](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-bjxvt)
- **The xtb program**, when you ran GFN2-xTB or GFN-FF with **xtb 6.8 compatible** conventions: C. Bannwarth et al., _WIREs Comput. Mol. Sci._ **11**, e1493 (2021), [doi:10.1002/wcms.1493](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.1493)

## By feature

| You used                                                    | Also cite                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Implicit solvent (ALPB or GBSA)                             | S. Ehlert, M. Stahn, S. Spicher, S. Grimme, _J. Chem. Theory Comput._ **17**, 4250 (2021), [doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.1c00471](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.1c00471); for g-xTB in a solvent, also the GFN2-xTB paper, whose solvation correction it uses                                                                                                                            |
| Optimize                                                    | Wang & Song 2016 (TRIC, the default), Baker et al. 1996 (delocalized coordinates), Lindh et al. 1995 (model Hessian), Banerjee et al. 1985 (RFO); if the run fell back to them, Grimme et al. 2017 (ANC) or Liu & Nocedal 1989 and Armijo 1966 (L-BFGS)                                                                                                                             |
| Conformer search                                            | Riniker & Landrum 2015 and Wang et al. 2020 (ETKDG), Butina 1999, the Optimize papers, and the method that minimized the starts: Halgren 1999 (MMFF94s, the default), Halgren 1996 (MMFF94), Rappé et al. 1992 (UFF, also used in place of MMFF for molecules with a metal), or the GFN-FF or GFN2-xTB paper above                                                                  |
| Frequencies and thermochemistry                             | Grimme 2012 (quasi-RRHO), Fernández-Ramos et al. 2007 (symmetry numbers)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| IR spectrum, orbital energies and coefficients              | nothing beyond the method                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Orbital surfaces in a figure                                | Lorensen & Cline 1987 and Doi & Koide 1991, Taubin 1995, Garland & Heckbert 1997 (Schneider 1990 for vector outlines)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Transition-state search (GFN2-xTB only, `reshiki --cli ts`) | Smidstrup et al. 2014 (IDPP), Henkelman et al. 2000 (CI-NEB) and Henkelman & Jónsson 2000 (tangent), Bitzek et al. 2006 (FIRE), Banerjee et al. 1985 and Baker 1986 (P-RFO), Bofill 1994, Page & McIver 1988 (IRC), Kolsbjerg et al. 2016 (AutoNEB-inspired midpoint recovery), McIver & Komornicki 1972 (saddle merit function), and the Optimize papers for the relaxed endpoints |
| Data read from ORCA, Gaussian and other programs' files     | ReShiki, and that program and its method instead of axtb (for ORCA 6, Neese 2025)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

The BibTeX keys (for example `Wang2016TRIC`) are the same in the file and in ReShiki's source code, so you can find where each method is implemented.
