The main use: get a good transition-state guess cheaply with xTB, then refine it with DFT in Gaussian or ORCA.

## How it works

1. Draw the reaction with an arrow and map the atoms (**Auto-map first** helps). Hydrogens that move but are not drawn are inferred and shown for you to confirm.
2. Choose **Find transition state (xTB)…** on the arrow. GFN2-xTB only, up to 120 atoms; intramolecular reactions and one-to-two or two-to-one reactions first.
3. ReShiki builds and optimizes both ends in 3D, interpolates a path between them, and refines it to a saddle point (climbing-image NEB, then a saddle-point search with the analytic Hessian).
4. It checks the result: exactly one imaginary frequency, and an IRC in both directions that leads to your reactant and product. If it leads somewhere else, it says where.

## What you will see

A **Path** tab with the energy profile (kcal/mol, reactant at 0), the barrier forward and back with a reminder that semi-empirical barriers are rough, and the imaginary mode animated on the model with forming and breaking bonds dashed.

## Export to Gaussian, ORCA and GAMESS

From the result, the export dialog writes:

- Gaussian: `Opt=(TS,CalcFC,NoEigenTest) Freq` on the guess, QST2 (reactant and product) and QST3 (plus the guess), or IRC.
- ORCA: `OptTS Freq` that reads the xTB Hessian from a `.hess` file (no first Hessian to compute), NEB-TS with the product, or IRC.
- GAMESS: `RUNTYP=SADPOINT`.
