ReShiki calculates frequencies from the analytic Hessian of the chosen xTB method, for molecules of up to 200 atoms.

## Run a frequency calculation

In **Predict → xTB**, choose the task:

- **Opt + freq** optimizes the molecule with Tight convergence, then calculates the frequencies at the optimized geometry. Use this for a new structure.
- **Frequencies** calculates them at the current 3D model, for a structure that is already optimized.

Then click **Run**. The results palette opens on **Summary**; choose its **Vibrations** tab.

- The list shows one row per mode with its frequency (cm⁻¹) and IR intensity (km/mol). Imaginary modes come first, in red, as negative numbers.
- Select a mode to animate it on the 3D model. **Pause** / **Play**, **Amplitude** and **Speed** control the animation.
- **Show arrows** draws the displacements on the model; **Add to drawing** adds them as ordinary pen paths you can restyle (one Undo step).
- **Export mode as XYZ movie…** writes the selected mode as a multi-frame XYZ file.
- The IR spectrum uses **Lorentzian** or **Gaussian** bands with the **FWHM** and **Scale factor** you set. Click a peak to select its mode.

## Put the IR spectrum in a figure

**Place in drawing** adds the spectrum next to the molecule as editable vector objects: the curve, axis, tick labels, peak labels and a caption such as `GFN2-xTB · scale 1.00 · FWHM 15 cm⁻¹ · Lorentzian`. It is one Undo step and exports to SVG, PDF and the other figure formats like any drawing. **Export SVG…** and **Export PDF…** save the spectrum on its own.

## Thermochemistry

Open **Thermochemistry** in the Vibrations tab for the zero-point vibrational energy (ZPVE), enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy and heat capacity, with the point group and symmetry number. For a molecule with isotope labels, the symmetry number counts only the rotations that map each atom onto one of the same element and isotope, at the geometry of the frequency calculation (the optimized one for **Opt + freq**): a symmetric D₂O has 2, HDO 1 and CD₄ 12. ReShiki uses the quasi-RRHO treatment that xtb uses by default: low-frequency modes blend toward free rotors, with the crossover at 50 cm⁻¹. Enter another temperature or pressure to recompute them without a new calculation.

## Imaginary frequencies

One or more imaginary frequencies mean the structure is not a minimum. **Push along mode and re-optimize** moves the structure 0.3 Å along the imaginary mode and runs **Opt + freq** again. The structure changes only if that optimization converges; Cancel leaves it as it was.

## File size

The file keeps the frequencies, IR intensities and thermochemistry. By default the normal-mode vectors are not saved: ReShiki recomputes them when you open the Vibrations tab, and shows them only if the recomputed frequencies match the saved ones, so a build without the xTB engine cannot show them. **Keep full results in this file** saves the vectors too.

## How good the numbers are

xTB frequencies are approximate. They reliably tell a minimum from a saddle point and give the shape of an IR spectrum; individual bands can be off by tens of cm⁻¹. For thermochemistry in a paper, recompute the frequencies with DFT; the [input export](/guide/calculation-files/) writes an **Opt + freq** input for Gaussian, ORCA or GAMESS from the xTB geometry.
