## Write a caption

Choose **Text** (**t**, lowercase), click the canvas, and type. Double-click a caption to edit it. Select a text range to change its font, size, bold/italic, subscript, superscript, or color. **Done** or **Cmd/Ctrl+Enter** applies; **Escape** cancels.

## Number atoms

For atom numbers, hydrogen placement, and stereochemistry labels, use **Properties → Labels & chemistry → Atom labels & numbering…**. Select an atom to change charge, isotope, or unpaired electrons under **Atom details**.

Atom numbers are placed automatically on the open side of their atom, clear of bonds, labels and captions. A longer custom number, such as `12a` or `Cα`, starts beside the atom and extends away from the structure. Drag a number with **Position numbers & stereo labels** to place it yourself; that position is kept.

Adding a charge to a skeletal carbon leaves the carbon label implicit. The charge remains visible at the vertex. Explicit carbon-label settings, isotope labels, and isolated atoms remain visible.

## Change OH to HO

You can choose which side of an oxygen atom displays its hydrogen—for example, to change **OH** to **HO**.

1. With **Select / move**, click the oxygen atom you want to adjust.
2. Open **Properties → Labels & chemistry → Atom labels & numbering…**.
3. Set the scope to **Selected atoms** and check that only one atom is in scope.
4. Under **Hydrogen position**, choose **Left** for **HO** or **Right** for **OH**.

Choose **Automatic** to let ReShiki determine the placement from the surrounding bonds. Keep **Show implied hydrogens** enabled to display the hydrogen.

This changes the label's appearance only. The bonds and stereochemistry stay the same. Use **Selected atoms** to adjust one hydroxyl; **Whole drawing** applies the setting throughout the drawing. **Cmd/Ctrl+Z** undoes a placement change.

## Shorten a group

Select an atom and press **Enter** to edit its label. **Automatic** recognizes elements and defined groups; **Chemical abbreviation** chooses a nickname explicitly, including names that conflict with element symbols. **Text label** keeps literal text such as M, L or X on a wildcard atom. Text labels do not imply an element, query or molecular formula.

A group's reverse spelling also makes the group: **MeO** gives OMe and **BnO** gives OBn, shown in whichever spelling suits the side the bond leaves from. **NC** and **OCN** make CN and NCO only on a left-hand end, where they read toward the bond; elsewhere they would mean an isocyanide or a cyanate, so they stay text. Full names such as **morpholino** or **acetyl** stay text; the editor suggests the label to type, such as "Did you mean Morph?".

Free text labels such as `M2+`, `R1` or `L2` are drawn with subscripted counts and a superscripted trailing charge, as in ChemDraw: `R1` appears as R₁, `M2+` as M²⁺, and `SO4 2-` as SO₄²⁻. The label keeps the text you typed, and the styling adds no chemical meaning.

Use **Properties → Labels & chemistry → Chemical abbreviations…** to replace a terminal group with a preset such as **OMe**, or contract a selected fragment. **Expand selected** restores its atoms for editing.

Defined groups such as **Boc** retain their full atom/bond graph for composition. **Cp** is cyclopentadienyl (C5H5−); **Cp\*** is pentamethylcyclopentadienyl (C10H15−). Their attachment handles are not counted as atoms. Metal charge still needs the correct explicit assignment.

Group labels default to **Automatic** alignment. Select groups and use the existing top **Left / Center / Right** buttons; the adjacent menu restores **Automatic** or selects **Above**. Captions share those buttons, and mixed selections update together. Alignment never changes the group's atoms or bonds. Above positions one nickname above its anchor rather than stacking arbitrary formula tokens.

## Type formulas and explicit hydrogens

In the atom label editor, type a supported formula such as **C2H5** or **OCH3**. Automatic mode creates a chemical group with real atoms; its digits display as subscripts. Select several atoms and press **Enter** to give that fragment a group name. Use Text label only when you want literal, chemically unspecified text.

An explicit **NH3** label retains three hydrogens. At a metal, its donor contact can be represented without changing it into NH2. These explicit hydrogens participate in molecular properties; labels are not merely decorative. This does not turn off chemical validation.

## Type a charge on an atom

Select an atom, press **Enter**, and type the element with its charge, such as `Pd2+`, `Pd+2`, `Na+` or `Cl-`. In **Automatic** mode, this sets the element and its charge, and the charge is drawn as a superscript. Charges go from -8 to +8.

A charged label gets no automatic hydrogens. Type the element alone to restore them. Choose **Text label** to keep the text exactly as typed.

ChemDraw reads `Pd2+` as two palladium atoms; ReShiki reads it as one Pd²⁺ because a single atom cannot be two atoms.

## Format part of an atom label

Select characters in the label box and press **Cmd+-** for subscript or **Cmd++** for superscript (**Ctrl** on Windows and Linux; **Cmd/Ctrl+=** also works). You can also use **X₂** and **X²** under the box. **Plain** (**Cmd/Ctrl+T**) removes the formatting. With nothing selected, the change applies to the whole label. Once you format characters, **Appearance** under the box shows the result.

In **Automatic** mode, `Pd` with a superscripted `2+` becomes a palladium atom with charge +2, and `NH` with a subscripted `4` and a superscripted `+` becomes ammonium. Other formatted text, such as M with a superscripted 2+ or R with a subscripted 1, stays a text label with your formatting. **Text label** always keeps the text and formatting as typed. Chemical abbreviations and group labels cannot be formatted.

To format a whole text label without opening the editor, select its atom and use **x₂** or **x²** in the Style bar.

The formatting is saved in the .rsk file and copied to ChemDraw files. Formatted text labels in ChemDraw files keep their formatting when you open them.

## Bond to a text label

You can draw a bond from a short caption, such as **Pd²⁺** typed with **Text**, as you would from an atom. Choose **Single bond** or another bond tool and drag from the caption's letters. The caption becomes an atom label and keeps its font, size, and color. **Pd²⁺** becomes a palladium atom with charge +2; other formatted text, such as M²⁺, stays a text label with its formatting.

Click the caption to add a bond in the default direction. A bond can also end on a caption. **Straight chain** and **Snaking chain** can start from a caption too. **Hydrogen bond** does not convert captions.

When you point at a caption with a bond tool, the status bar says whether it can become an atom label. Captions with more than one line or more than 32 characters, molecule names, and reaction conditions stay captions. One **Undo** restores the caption and removes the new bond.

## Choose where a bond meets a text label

A bond meets the attachment atom inside a multi-atom text label, not the middle of the label. In **MeO**, **AcS**, **BnO** or **SPh** the bond meets the O or S; nicknames such as Me, Ac, Bn and Ph, hydrogens and counts never take the bond, even when they spell an element symbol (Ac). When a label names several atoms, the bond meets the one nearest it: **CO2Me** attaches at C with the bond on its left, and **MeO2C** attaches at C with the bond on its right. Text that names no atom, such as **Boc** or **M**, stays centered, and so does text spelled for the other side, such as **MeO** with its bond on the left, so the bond is not hidden behind Me. Type **OMe** for that direction. A text label inside a chain, with two or more bonds, also stays centered unless you choose otherwise. Chemical groups such as **OAc** or **CO2Me** typed in **Automatic** mode always attach at their real anchor atom and switch to their reverse spelling (AcO, MeO2C) as needed.

To choose differently, select the atom and open **Properties → Labels & chemistry → Atom labels & numbering…**. With **Selected atoms** as the scope, use **Bond attachment (text labels)**:

- **Automatic** follows the bonds.
- **Flush left** attaches at the first atom of the label; **Flush right** at the last.
- **Centered** centers the whole label on the atom.
- **Character N** attaches at exactly that character, for example the M of MeO.

The choice is saved in the .rsk file and applies to the canvas and to SVG, PDF and PNG exports. When you copy to ChemDraw, flush left, centered and flush right are written as label justification, and they are read back from ChemDraw clipboard data and files. Saving a drawing with text labels as a ChemDraw file is not supported yet; a chosen character has no ChemDraw equivalent and is kept only in .rsk files. Editing the label text returns a chosen character to Automatic. **Use drawing label defaults** also resets it.

## Attach to several atoms

Select the target atoms, right-click → **Attachment points…**, and choose **Multi-center attachment** for simultaneous participation, such as η6-arene or η3-allyl. **Variable attachment** represents alternative attachment positions. Draw a bond from the new point to a metal or substituent. These are distinct from a plain dummy atom or drawing centroid.

Anonymous dummy and attachment handles remain visible in the editor but disappear from figures and image copies. Explicit labels remain visible. Native editing retains target IDs; supported editable exchange uses CDXML/CDX attachment nodes or V3000 ALL/ANY lists. See [the verified scope and limitations](/developer/chemdraw-attachment-comparison/).
