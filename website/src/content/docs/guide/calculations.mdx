ReShiki can calculate the energy of a molecule and optimize its 3D geometry with the semi-empirical xTB methods. The calculation runs on your computer in a separate process, so you can keep drawing, and a crash cannot take the drawing with it.

## What xTB is good for

xTB methods are fast approximations to quantum chemistry from Stefan Grimme's group. They give good geometries for organic and organometallic molecules in seconds, and useful trends for energies, charges and frontier orbitals. They do not replace DFT:

- Geometries: good. Use them as figures, or as starting structures for DFT.
- Relative energies: semi-quantitative. Compare conformers or isomers; check reaction energies with DFT.
- Frequencies: approximate, but enough to tell a minimum from a saddle point.
- Orbitals: qualitative (minimal basis).

The results palette repeats this in one line under every result.

## Methods

| Method                    | Use it for                                                                          | Not available                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **GFN2-xTB** (default)    | Most molecules, elements up to radon (Z = 86)                                       | Lanthanide f electrons (they stay in the core)                        |
| **g-xTB** (version 2.0.1) | Energies closer to DFT; in solvent, the GFN2-xTB solvation energy is added          | Transition-state search (planned for GFN2-xTB only)                   |
| **GFN-FF**                | Large molecules and metal complexes; a force field, the fastest; closed shells only | Orbitals, bond orders, solvent, SCF settings, transition-state search |

Solvents use the ALPB model: water, methanol, DMSO, chloroform, toluene and others. The card turns off what the chosen method cannot do and says why under the field.

## Run a calculation

1. Select the molecule. If the drawing holds only one molecule, you can leave the selection empty.
2. Open **Predict → xTB**, or search the command palette for **Calculate (xTB)…**.
3. Choose the **Task** and the **Method**. **Energy** and **Optimize** are described here; **Opt + freq** and **Frequencies** on [Frequencies and IR spectra](/guide/calculation-frequencies/), **Conformers** on [Conformer search](/guide/calculation-conformers/). Every GFN2-xTB and g-xTB run shows its orbitals in the results' [Orbitals tab](/guide/calculation-orbitals/).
4. Check **Charge** and **Unpaired electrons**. They start from the charges and radicals you drew, and you can change them. Once the molecule has a 3D model, the line below checks the electron count, for example `40 electrons · multiplicity 1`.
5. Pick a **Solvent**, or leave **None (gas phase)**.
6. Click **Run**.

In builds with xTB, open **3D model ▾** in the row above the canvas (or **⋯** when the row is narrow). Choose **Optimize with xTB (quantum, GFN2)** or **Opt + freq (xTB)** to start at once. **Force-field 3D model (MMFF94s)…** opens a preview, and **More xTB settings…** opens **Predict → xTB**. Builds without xTB keep **3D optimize…**.

To start from the drawing, right-click the molecule and choose **Calculate (xTB) → Energy** or **Optimize**. The card's other settings apply. Press ⌘R (Ctrl+R on Linux) to run the selected molecule with the card's current settings.

If the molecule has no 3D model yet, **Run** first makes one with MMFF94s (for structures MMFF94s cannot handle, such as many metal complexes, make or open a 3D model first); if the drawing changed since the model was made, it updates the model first. Either is its own Undo step. The 2D drawing never moves.

While it runs, the card shows the step, the energy change and the time, for example `Optimizing · step 12 · ΔE −2.1 × 10⁻⁵ Eh · 3.2 s`, and the 3D model follows the optimization. **Cancel** stops at once and applies no xTB result (a [conformer search](/guide/calculation-conformers/) keeps the conformers already finished). A 3D model made or updated for this run stays; Undo reverses it.

### Advanced settings

- **Accuracy** and **Max SCF iterations** tighten or loosen the SCF.
- **Electronic temp.** smears the orbital occupations: default 300 K for GFN2-xTB, 0 K for g-xTB. Raise it when the SCF does not converge.
- **xtb 6.8 compatible** uses the numerical conventions of the reference programs instead of axtb's own: those of xtb 6.8 for GFN2-xTB and GFN-FF, and of the g-xTB 2.0.1 program for g-xTB. The result records the choice.
- **Threads**: **Auto** uses the computer's cores, up to 8.
- **Optimizer convergence**: **Normal** or **Tight**.

### Hold atoms in place

Select atoms, turn on **Hold selected atoms fixed**, then **Run**. The optimizer keeps their coordinates.

## Read the results

Results open in the **xTB results** palette at the bottom of the window, like the NMR palette.

- **Summary**: total energy (Eh), HOMO–LUMO gap (eV), dipole (D), atomic charges, bond orders of at least 0.1, orbital levels from HOMO−2 to LUMO+2, the optimization (steps and RMSD from the start), the RMS gradient and the run time. Rows without data are left out.
- **Steps** (optimizations): the energy at every step. Click a step to preview it on the 3D model; **Use this geometry** applies it (asking first if a stereocentre or double bond changed), and Esc ends the preview.
- **Log**: what ran, with the settings and SCF iterations, ready to copy into a lab notebook.
- **Show as labels** writes the energy under the molecule and the charges next to its atoms on the canvas. The labels stay on screen; they are not part of exports. **Copy table** and **Export…** give the Summary rows as tab-separated text; the bond-order and orbital tables are not included.
- **Dock right** moves the palette into the Predict tab. **Collapse** folds it to its header (**Expand** opens it again); **Close** hides it until the next result or **Show results** on the card.

When the HOMO–LUMO gap is below 0.1 eV, a warning appears under the header: _Near-degenerate electronic structure: results can differ between computers. Try a higher electronic temperature or more unpaired electrons._

## Optimized geometries

When an optimization finishes, ReShiki first compares stereocentres and double-bond geometry with the drawing. If none changed, the optimized geometry replaces the molecule's 3D model as one Undo step (`Applied xTB geometry · ⌘Z undoes`). If one changed, the check could not run, or an edit is still open, nothing is applied until you choose **Apply anyway** or **Discard**. The 2D drawing does not move.

- An optimization that reaches the step limit before converging can still apply its last geometry, and says `Stopped after N steps`.
- If you changed the molecule's atoms or bonds, or its 3D coordinates, while it ran, the result is kept but the model is not moved.

If you later change the molecule's atoms or bonds, its results stay readable but greyed. **Recalculate** appears when the card can repeat that calculation.

## When something goes wrong

Errors appear in the palette, with an action where one helps:

- **The SCF did not converge.** If the drawing is unchanged and the run used less than 1000 K, the palette offers **Retry at 1000 K**.
- An element the method has no parameters for: the message names the atom. GFN2-xTB covers elements up to radon; g-xTB and GFN-FF go further.
- Too many atoms: the message gives the limit.
- **The calculation stopped unexpectedly. Your drawing is safe.** → **Copy details** for a bug report, and **Run again** if the drawing is unchanged.

## Limits

- Energy and optimization: up to 500 atoms.
- Frequencies and conformer searches: up to 200 atoms. Planned: transition states up to 120.
- One molecule per calculation: selecting some of its atoms calculates the whole molecule. To calculate a fragment, copy it into a separate molecule first.

## File size

Results are saved in the `.rsk` file with the molecule and kept small on purpose: the method and SCF settings, the starting and final geometries, the energy of every step, charges, dipole, bond orders and orbital energies. The geometries of intermediate steps and the gradients are left out; after reopening, clicking such a step says it is not saved in the file. To keep them, turn on **Keep full results in this file** before you save. The palette footer shows **Saved in file** for each result.

- **Keep full results in this file** also saves the step geometries and gradients of that result, at the size it shows.
- The threads, optimizer convergence, held atoms and run summary (run time, SCF iterations) are saved with the result, so **Recalculate** after reopening uses the same calculation settings on the current geometry (a conformer search takes its search settings from the card). The Log's per-step energies and times are kept only while the app is open.
- When the results in a document exceed 5 MB, Save asks once. If some xTB results keep full data, it offers **Make lighter**, which drops that data; otherwise it offers **Save**. Data imported from other programs cannot be recomputed, so it stays.

## Platforms and licence

xTB calculations are in the macOS and Linux downloads. Windows builds do not include them yet; they will once Windows code signing covers the calculation engine.

The engine is [axtb](https://github.com/atomsforhumanity/axtb), a Rust implementation of the xTB methods, used through ReShiki's fork. axtb is licensed under the GNU Lesser General Public License, version 3 or later (LGPL-3.0-or-later); ReShiki's own code stays MIT OR Apache-2.0.
